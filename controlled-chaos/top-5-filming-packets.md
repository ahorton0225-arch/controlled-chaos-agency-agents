# Controlled Chaos Top 5 Filming Packets

Source: `controlled-chaos/test-run-ad-ideas.md` top 5 filming priorities.

Production rule for all packets: film vertically on a phone with one person, a gym corner, product tub, whiteboard or index cards, marker, and a phone/laptop quiz screen. Keep the product angle visible through action, not lecture.

---

## 1. Founder Whiteboard: Comment Your Split

### 1. Video title
Comment Your Split: Formula Priority Whiteboard

### 2. Goal of the video
Turn comments into future content while showing that Controlled Chaos builds from training structure, caffeine tolerance, and goals.

### 3. Target viewer
Powerbuilders, college lifters, and serious recreational lifters who know their split and want the formula to fit how they train.

### 4. Core objection or desire
Desire: “I want my pre-workout to fit my actual training, not just be a loud scoop.”

### 5. Why this idea should get views
The hook asks the viewer to participate immediately and the whiteboard creates a fast visual payoff.

### 6. Why this idea should get comments/shares
It invites viewers to drop their split and caffeine tolerance for a formula-priority reply.

### 7. Why this idea can drive sales
It demonstrates the quiz logic in public: split plus tolerance plus goal becomes formula priorities.

### 8. Exact 0–15 second timeline
- **0–1s:** Close-up of whiteboard: “COMMENT YOUR SPLIT.”
- **1–3s:** Founder points at a sample comment card: “Upper/lower. Heavy top set. Medium caffeine.”
- **3–6s:** Write three inputs: split, goal, tolerance.
- **6–10s:** Write formula priorities: focus support, controlled energy, pump support.
- **10–15s:** Hold tub beside board and say CTA.

### 9. Exact spoken script
“Drop your split and caffeine tolerance. Example: upper/lower, heavy top set first, medium caffeine, hates crash. I’m not building that like a random arm-day scoop. Priority one: focus support. Priority two: controlled energy. Priority three: pump support for accessories. That’s the point of Controlled Chaos — your formula starts with how you train.”

### 10. Exact shot list
1. Whiteboard title shot.
2. Hand places sample comment card on board.
3. Founder writes “Upper/Lower.”
4. Founder writes “Heavy top set.”
5. Founder writes “Medium caffeine.”
6. Founder writes priority list.
7. Tub beside board.
8. Phone quiz screen close-up.

### 11. Camera framing for each shot
1. Tight close-up on board title.
2. Over-the-shoulder board angle.
3. Chest-up founder with board visible.
4. Close-up marker writing.
5. Close-up product tub beside final board.
6. Tight phone screen shot for quiz moment.

### 12. Text overlays for each moment
- **0–1s:** “COMMENT YOUR SPLIT”
- **1–3s:** “Upper/Lower + Top Set + Medium Caffeine”
- **3–6s:** “Inputs first.”
- **6–10s:** “Formula priorities: focus → controlled energy → pump”
- **10–15s:** “Build yours.”

### 13. Props needed
Whiteboard or poster board, marker, product tub, phone with quiz screen, one handwritten comment card.

### 14. Location setup
Gym wall, empty rack corner, or garage gym wall with enough quiet for clean audio.

### 15. B-roll needed
Marker writing, tub beside board, quick close-up of quiz screen, optional rack/barbell shot.

### 16. Editing instructions
Use jump cuts on each written input. Keep every shot under two seconds. Add punchy captions. Cut dead air between marker movements.

### 17. Sound/music direction
Low-volume aggressive gym beat or no music with crisp spoken audio. Marker squeak can stay if it feels real.

### 18. Caption
“Comment your split + caffeine tolerance. I’ll pick the formula priorities.”

### 19. CTA
“Comment yours or build your formula.”

### 20. Comment-bait line
“Drop: split + training time + caffeine tolerance.”

### 21. Five alternate hooks
1. “Your split tells me more than your favorite pre-workout does.”
2. “Comment your split. I’ll build the formula priorities.”
3. “Upper/lower with heavy top sets? Different build.”
4. “This is why one scoop for every workout makes no sense.”
5. “Give me your lift schedule. I’ll show you the missing formula questions.”

### 22. Three shorter 7-second versions
1. “Upper/lower, heavy top set, medium caffeine. Priorities: focus, controlled energy, pump. Build around the lift.”
2. “Comment your split. I’ll tell you what your formula should prioritize before your next session.”
3. “Your pre should start with three inputs: split, goal, tolerance. Controlled Chaos builds from that.”

### 23. One paid-ad version
“Most pre-workouts ask one question: do you want energy? Controlled Chaos asks what you train, when you train, and what you tolerate. Take the quiz and build your formula around your actual split.”

### 24. One organic/founder version
“Comment your split. I’m going to keep doing these formula-priority breakdowns until every random scoop in this gym feels nervous.”

### 25. One influencer/UGC version
Creator films their own split on paper, circles training time and caffeine tolerance, then says: “This is why I wanted a formula built around how I actually train.”

### 26. Claim-safety notes
Use “support,” “priority,” and “built around.” Do not promise strength increases, PRs, sleep outcomes, or stimulant safety.

### 27. What would make this video bad or corny
Overexplaining the brand, saying “revolutionary,” using fake excitement, or spending 10 seconds pointing at the tub instead of the whiteboard mechanism.

### 28. Final filming checklist
- [ ] Whiteboard title is readable.
- [ ] Comment card includes split, goal, tolerance.
- [ ] Quiz screen appears for at least one second.
- [ ] Product tub appears after the logic is clear.
- [ ] CTA asks for comments and formula build.
- [ ] No unsupported performance claims.

## 2. Leg Day Formula Fantasy Draft

### 1. Video title
Leg Day Formula Fantasy Draft

### 2. Goal of the video
Show that leg day formula priorities are different from generic max-stim pre-workout positioning.

### 3. Target viewer
College lifters, bodybuilders, and pump-focused lifters running push/pull/legs or high-volume leg days.

### 4. Core objection or desire
Desire: “I want energy, pump, and endurance for leg day without just nuking myself with caffeine.”

### 5. Why this idea should get views
Fantasy draft format creates a familiar ranking game with a surprise first pick.

### 6. Why this idea should get comments/shares
Viewers will argue about first pick: pump, endurance, focus, or caffeine.

### 7. Why this idea can drive sales
It makes formula personalization visual: the product starts with workout demands, not a one-size-fits-all scoop.

### 8. Exact 0–15 second timeline
- **0–1s:** Board close-up: “LEG DAY DRAFT.”
- **1–3s:** Founder says: “First pick is not caffeine.”
- **3–6s:** Write “1. Pump support.”
- **6–10s:** Write “2. Endurance support” and show quick leg machine/rack B-roll.
- **10–15s:** Write “3. Moderate energy” and reveal tub/quiz screen.

### 9. Exact spoken script
“Leg day formula draft. First pick is not more caffeine. First pick: pump support. Second pick: endurance support, because walking lunges do not care how loud the label is. Third pick: moderate energy. If you train legs after class, the formula should know that before it gets built.”

### 10. Exact shot list
1. Whiteboard draft board.
2. Founder writes “Round 1.”
3. Founder writes “Pump support.”
4. Quick B-roll: squat rack, leg press, or dumbbells near legs.
5. Founder writes “Endurance support.”
6. Founder writes “Moderate energy.”
7. Tub and phone quiz screen final shot.

### 11. Camera framing for each shot
1. Tight whiteboard close-up.
2. Medium shot with founder and board.
3. Close-up marker writing each draft pick.
4. Low-angle gym equipment B-roll.
5. Tight tub-and-phone shot at the end.

### 12. Text overlays for each moment
- **0–1s:** “LEG DAY DRAFT”
- **1–3s:** “First pick is NOT more caffeine.”
- **3–6s:** “1. Pump support”
- **6–10s:** “2. Endurance support”
- **10–15s:** “3. Moderate energy — build around the lift.”

### 13. Props needed
Whiteboard/poster board, marker, product tub, phone quiz screen, gym rack or leg equipment.

### 14. Location setup
Film beside squat rack, leg press, or any leg-day-looking station. Keep background intense but not crowded.

### 15. B-roll needed
Squat rack, leg press, walking lunges, shoes stepping toward rack, tub on bench.

### 16. Editing instructions
Use draft-pick sound effects or quick whoosh cuts. Add a subtle “draft pick” graphic if easy. Do not over-edit.

### 17. Sound/music direction
Sports-draft style drum hit or gym beat. Keep voice louder than music.

### 18. Caption
“Leg day draft board. What’s your first pick?”

### 19. CTA
“Build your formula before your next leg day.”

### 20. Comment-bait line
“First pick for leg day: pump, endurance, focus, or caffeine?”

### 21. Five alternate hooks
1. “First overall pick for leg day is not caffeine.”
2. “If leg day had a formula draft, this is my board.”
3. “Your leg day pre-workout needs a draft strategy.”
4. “Caffeine fell to round three. Fight me.”
5. “Building a leg day formula like it’s draft night.”

### 22. Three shorter 7-second versions
1. “Leg day draft: pump support first, endurance second, moderate energy third. Build around the lift.”
2. “If caffeine is your first leg-day pick every time, your formula skipped context.”
3. “Leg day after class? Pump, endurance, controlled energy. That’s the build.”

### 23. One paid-ad version
“Leg day is not the same as an arm pump day or conditioning day. Controlled Chaos uses your quiz answers to prioritize the formula around how you train. Build your custom pre-workout.”

### 24. One organic/founder version
“I know the high-stim crowd is going to yell, but caffeine is not my first pick for every leg day build.”

### 25. One influencer/UGC version
Creator shows their leg day written on a card, drafts their priorities, then says: “This is the formula logic I wanted before my next leg day.”

### 26. Claim-safety notes
Do not say it guarantees better endurance, bigger pumps, or better performance. Use “support,” “priority,” and “built around.”

### 27. What would make this video bad or corny
Making it a generic “leg day pre-workout” ad, yelling at the camera, or pretending caffeine is evil instead of showing a tradeoff.

### 28. Final filming checklist
- [ ] Draft board is visible in first second.
- [ ] First pick surprises viewer.
- [ ] Leg-day context is obvious.
- [ ] Tub and quiz screen appear after draft logic.
- [ ] Caption asks for viewer’s first pick.
- [ ] No guaranteed pump/endurance claims.

## 3. Same Scoop, Three Different Workouts

### 1. Video title
Same Scoop, Three Different Workouts

### 2. Goal of the video
Make the one-size-fits-all problem obvious in one visual comparison.

### 3. Target viewer
Gym-goers who train different session types during the week and suspect a single generic formula is not ideal.

### 4. Core objection or desire
Objection: “Is custom pre-workout actually different, or just branding?”

### 5. Why this idea should get views
The three-card visual is immediately understandable and creates a simple “wait, true” moment.

### 6. Why this idea should get comments/shares
Viewers can comment which workout they are doing today or argue which priority belongs to which session.

### 7. Why this idea can drive sales
It explains Controlled Chaos differentiation faster than a product claim: different workouts create different formula priorities.

### 8. Exact 0–15 second timeline
- **0–1s:** Three cards hit table: “Deadlift,” “Arms,” “Conditioning.”
- **1–3s:** Put one card over all three: “Same scoop?”
- **3–6s:** Pull it away and say: “That’s the weird part.”
- **6–10s:** Write different priorities under each workout.
- **10–15s:** Show quiz screen and tub: “Controlled Chaos starts with the workout.”

### 9. Exact spoken script
“Deadlift day. Arm day. Conditioning. Same scoop for all three? That’s the weird part. This one needs focus and controlled energy. This one wants pump support. This one needs endurance and controlled energy. Controlled Chaos starts with the workout before it builds the formula.”

### 10. Exact shot list
1. Cards slapped onto bench/table.
2. “Same scoop?” card placed over them.
3. Founder pulls card away.
4. Write priorities beneath each card.
5. Phone quiz screen.
6. Tub beside final three-card layout.

### 11. Camera framing for each shot
1. Top-down table/bench shot.
2. Tight card close-ups.
3. Medium founder shot for one sentence.
4. Top-down final layout.
5. Tight phone and tub close-up.

### 12. Text overlays for each moment
- **0–1s:** “Deadlift. Arms. Conditioning.”
- **1–3s:** “Same scoop?”
- **3–6s:** “That’s the weird part.”
- **6–10s:** “Different session = different priorities.”
- **10–15s:** “Build around the workout.”

### 13. Props needed
Index cards, marker, product tub, phone quiz screen, bench/table.

### 14. Location setup
Use a flat bench, plyo box, or gym table. Top-down filming is easiest.

### 15. B-roll needed
Optional quick clips: deadlift bar, curl dumbbells, bike/treadmill/conditioning area.

### 16. Editing instructions
Use fast card slaps as cuts. Keep top-down layout clean. Add captions directly above each card.

### 17. Sound/music direction
Card slap sound effects, low-volume beat, no overdramatic voiceover.

### 18. Caption
“Which one are you training today?”

### 19. CTA
“Take the quiz and build around your workout.”

### 20. Comment-bait line
“Comment today’s workout: deadlift, arms, legs, push, pull, or conditioning.”

### 21. Five alternate hooks
1. “One scoop for these three workouts is crazy when you think about it.”
2. “Deadlift day and arm day should not be treated the same.”
3. “Here’s the fastest way to explain Controlled Chaos.”
4. “Three workouts. Three different formula priorities.”
5. “If your pre ignores the workout, what is it even building for?”

### 22. Three shorter 7-second versions
1. “Deadlift, arms, conditioning. Same scoop? Weird. Different workouts need different priorities.”
2. “This is Controlled Chaos in one shot: workout first, formula second.”
3. “Deadlift needs focus. Arms want pump. Conditioning needs endurance. Build around that.”

### 23. One paid-ad version
“Your training week is not one-size-fits-all. Controlled Chaos asks what you train, what you want, and what you tolerate, then builds formula priorities around that.”

### 24. One organic/founder version
“This is the simplest way I can explain why we built Controlled Chaos. Your workouts are different. Your formula should start there.”

### 25. One influencer/UGC version
Creator lays out three workouts from their actual week, then shows how they would want different formula priorities for each.

### 26. Claim-safety notes
Do not claim better lifts or performance outcomes. Keep language to “priorities,” “support,” and “build around.”

### 27. What would make this video bad or corny
Turning it into a rant, insulting viewers, or using vague “custom for you” language without showing the cards.

### 28. Final filming checklist
- [ ] Three workout cards are readable.
- [ ] “Same scoop?” visual appears by second 3.
- [ ] Different priorities are written on screen.
- [ ] Quiz/tub reveal happens only after logic lands.
- [ ] CTA is one sentence.
- [ ] No unsupported claims.

## 4. High-Stim Bro Courtroom Trial

### 1. Video title
High-Stim Bro Courtroom Trial

### 2. Goal of the video
Use a funny courtroom mechanism to show that training time and caffeine tolerance matter.

### 3. Target viewer
Late-night lifters, high-stim fans, and gym-goers who joke about being stimmed out.

### 4. Core objection or desire
Desire: “I want the pre-workout to hit, but I do not want the formula to ignore my tolerance and training time.”

### 5. Why this idea should get views
Courtroom framing creates instant conflict and lets one person play judge/prosecutor/founder.

### 6. Why this idea should get comments/shares
People will tag high-stim friends and argue whether max stim at night is unhinged or normal.

### 7. Why this idea can drive sales
It turns caffeine tolerance from a boring quiz question into the reason Controlled Chaos asks better questions.

### 8. Exact 0–15 second timeline
- **0–1s:** Shaker slams like gavel: “Court is now in session.”
- **1–3s:** Card: “Charge: max stim before 9 p.m. arm day.”
- **3–6s:** Evidence cards: training time, goal, tolerance.
- **6–10s:** Founder says the formula should not start with max stim.
- **10–15s:** Verdict card plus tub: “Build around the session.”

### 9. Exact spoken script
“Court is now in session. Charge: taking max stim before a 9 p.m. arm day. Evidence: training time, 9 p.m. Goal, pump. Tolerance, medium. The formula should not start with ‘how much caffeine can we stuff in here?’ Controlled Chaos asks the questions before it builds. Verdict: build around the session.”

### 10. Exact shot list
1. Shaker gavel slam.
2. Product tub as defendant.
3. Charge card close-up.
4. Three evidence cards.
5. Founder points to “medium tolerance.”
6. Verdict card.
7. Tub and quiz screen final.

### 11. Camera framing for each shot
1. Tight shaker close-up.
2. Low-angle tub on bench.
3. Close-up of cards.
4. Medium founder shot with cards.
5. Tight verdict card.
6. End on tub and phone screen.

### 12. Text overlays for each moment
- **0–1s:** “GYM COURT”
- **1–3s:** “Charge: max stim at 9 p.m.”
- **3–6s:** “Evidence: time + goal + tolerance”
- **6–10s:** “Formula should ask first.”
- **10–15s:** “Verdict: build around the session.”

### 13. Props needed
Shaker, index cards, marker, product tub, phone quiz screen, bench.

### 14. Location setup
Gym bench or locker-room-style corner. Product tub sits where a defendant would sit.

### 15. B-roll needed
Optional arm curl shot, clock/phone showing 9 p.m., close-up of quiz tolerance input.

### 16. Editing instructions
Use gavel sound, quick zoom on “guilty?” or “verdict,” and hard cuts between evidence cards.

### 17. Sound/music direction
Courtroom gavel sound plus low gym beat. Avoid goofy circus music.

### 18. Caption
“High stim at 9 p.m.: guilty or innocent?”

### 19. CTA
“Take the quiz and build around your session.”

### 20. Comment-bait line
“Be honest: what time do you take pre?”

### 21. Five alternate hooks
1. “The high-stim bro has been summoned to gym court.”
2. “Your honor, he took max stim at 9 p.m.”
3. “This pre-workout habit is on trial.”
4. “The defendant claims caffeine is a personality.”
5. “Gym court case #1: late-night max stim.”

### 22. Three shorter 7-second versions
1. “Max stim at 9 p.m. arm day? Gym court says ask time, goal, and tolerance first.”
2. “Verdict: your formula should know when you train.”
3. “Charge: ignoring caffeine tolerance. Sentence: take the quiz.”

### 23. One paid-ad version
“Controlled Chaos asks about training time, goals, and caffeine tolerance before building your formula priorities. Build a pre-workout around your session, not a random label.”

### 24. One organic/founder version
“I am not anti-caffeine. I am anti-pretending every lifter at every time of day needs the same caffeine strategy.”

### 25. One influencer/UGC version
Creator puts their own late-night routine “on trial,” shows their quiz tolerance answer, and explains why they wanted a session-specific build.

### 26. Claim-safety notes
Do not promise better sleep or safer stimulation. Say “tolerance input,” “training time,” and “formula priority.”

### 27. What would make this video bad or corny
Overacting the judge voice, making medical claims about caffeine, or turning it into “caffeine bad” instead of “context matters.”

### 28. Final filming checklist
- [ ] Shaker gavel opens the video.
- [ ] Charge card is readable by second 3.
- [ ] Evidence cards include time, goal, tolerance.
- [ ] Verdict connects to quiz/formula logic.

# Final filming recommendations

## Film first
**Founder Whiteboard: Comment Your Split** — it is the fastest to shoot, easiest to repeat, and best for creating a comment-driven content loop.

## Easiest
**Same Scoop, Three Different Workouts** — it can be filmed entirely top-down with index cards, marker, quiz screen, and product tub.

## Highest sales potential
**Same Scoop, Three Different Workouts** — it explains the core Controlled Chaos difference in one visual: workouts are different, so formula priorities should start there.

## Highest comment potential
**Founder Whiteboard: Comment Your Split** — the entire format asks viewers to submit their split, training time, and caffeine tolerance.

## Best for an influencer
**Pre-Workout Loadout Screen: Late-Night Upper Day** — creators can adapt it to their own workout, training time, and preferences while keeping the visual mechanism intact.
