
# Controlled Chaos Target Customer

## Primary segments
1. **College lifter:** wants energy, taste, value, identity, gym status, and shareable content. Objections: price, shipping, skepticism about AI.
2. **Bodybuilder/pump chaser:** wants pump, focus, endurance, and formulas that fit hypertrophy days. Objections: ingredient transparency and dosing confidence.
3. **Powerbuilder/strength lifter:** wants aggression, focus, strength-session support, less fluff. Objections: hates influencer hype and proprietary-blend vibes.
4. **Athlete/conditioning customer:** wants endurance, clean energy, less crash, and sport-compatible routines. Objections: overstimulation and banned-claim anxiety.
5. **Serious recreational lifter:** wants to look better, train harder, and feel like the product was made for their routine. Objections: “Is this just a gimmick?”

## Motivations
- Better sessions, better pump, better focus, better consistency, better taste.
- Identity: “I train seriously enough to need my own setup.”
- Control: caffeine tolerance, pump vs focus emphasis, workout split, flavor preferences.

## Objections to overcome
- “AI supplement sounds fake.” Show the quiz logic and training-specific decisions.
- “Custom means expensive.” Explain value through fit, not novelty.
- “All pres are the same.” Compare scenarios, not brands.
- “I do not trust supplement claims.” Use transparent, restrained language.

## Language and content triggers
Use gym-native language: leg day, top set, pump, stim, crash, dry scoop culture, PR, bro split, push/pull/legs, delts, back day, conditioning, late lift. Triggers: roasts, audits, tier lists, blind tests, fantasy drafts, founder experiments, coach whiteboards, courtroom trials, street interviews.

## Quiz inputs that should appear in content
- Training style: bodybuilding, powerbuilding, strength, athletics/conditioning, general gym performance.
- Split: push/pull/legs, upper/lower, bro split, full body, sport practice plus lifts.
- Session context: early morning, after class, late-night, heavy top set, high-volume pump work, conditioning.
- Tolerance/preferences: low/medium/high stim, pump vs focus vs endurance emphasis, flavor preference, crash sensitivity.

## Objection language to mirror
- “AI sounds gimmicky.”
- “I do not want some random proprietary blend.”
- “High stim wrecks me.”
- “Most pre-workouts taste like battery acid.”
- “I train late; I cannot be wired until 3 a.m.”
- “I need pump for hypertrophy, not just caffeine.”
