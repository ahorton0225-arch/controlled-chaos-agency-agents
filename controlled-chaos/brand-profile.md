# Controlled Chaos Brand Profile

## Core identity
- **Name:** Controlled Chaos
- **Product:** custom AI-generated pre-workout built from quiz answers about training style, split, goals, caffeine tolerance, flavor and performance preferences.
- **Tagline:** Engineered by AI. Perfected by You.
- **Position:** the anti-one-size-fits-all pre-workout for serious lifters who train differently.
- **Belief:** the person training legs after class, the powerbuilder chasing a top set, and the athlete doing conditioning should not be sold the same scoop.

## Visual and voice
Black, red, gunmetal, premium, intense, gym-first. Voice is confident, sharp, clever, direct, high-energy and allergic to corporate filler. Say the thing plainly. Make the product feel engineered, not magical.

## Messaging rules
1. Always tie personalization to a real training context: leg day, push day, late-night lift, high-stim tolerance, pump chasing, endurance circuits.
2. Avoid unsupported medical or extreme performance claims. Use language like **designed for**, **built around**, **supports**, **dialed for**.
3. Never let “AI” be the whole idea. AI is the engineering mechanism; the lifter’s training is the story.
4. The CTA should be direct: **Build your formula**, **Take the quiz**, **Get your custom pre-workout**.
5. Every creative concept needs a visible mechanism: skit, conflict, test, audit, draft, debate, challenge, breakdown, or demonstration.

## Proof standards
- Show the quiz/formula logic visually whenever possible: split → goal → tolerance → preference → formula priority.
- Treat AI as an engineering assistant, not a miracle claim. The customer’s training data is the reason the formula changes.
- If a claim cannot be shown, sourced, or phrased as support, cut it.

## Controlled Chaos idea test
A good idea should make a lifter say: “That is exactly my training situation.” If the idea only says “custom,” “AI,” or “better pre-workout,” it fails.
