# Rejected ideas

## 11. AI Built My Pre-Workout — rejected
- **Reason:** AI is the entire hook, not the mechanism. It sounds like weak AI copy and could apply to any AI supplement brand.
- **Fix direction:** Rebuild as a visible quiz/formula decision for a named training scenario.

## 12. Stop Taking Random Pre-Workout — rejected
- **Reason:** Generic supplement scold. No character, conflict, setting, or visual mechanism.
- **Fix direction:** Convert into a courtroom trial, formula audit, or “same scoop, different workouts” comparison.

## 13. This Pre Gives You Insane Pumps — rejected
- **Reason:** Unsupported and overhyped supplement claim. Also too category-generic.
- **Fix direction:** Say “built to prioritize pump support for high-volume hypertrophy days” if substantiated by product logic.

## 14. Take the Quiz and Get Personalized Pre — rejected
- **Reason:** CTA masquerading as an idea. No entertainment value.
- **Fix direction:** Show the quiz through a loadout screen, game show, or whiteboard formula build.

## 15. Best Pre-Workout for Serious Lifters — rejected
- **Reason:** Generic “best” claim with no proof, no audience specificity, and no Controlled Chaos mechanism.
- **Fix direction:** Make it about one serious lifter profile and one formula tradeoff.

## 16. Before/After: Guaranteed PR Day — rejected
- **Reason:** Unsupported performance guarantee and risky supplement claim.
- **Fix direction:** Replace with a non-guaranteed training diary focused on energy/focus/pump support language.

## 17. Revolutionary AI Supplement Changes Everything — rejected
- **Reason:** Corporate, vague, and AI-first. Does not sound like gym culture.
- **Fix direction:** Use founder desk cam or formula patch notes to show what changed and why.

## 18. Gym Bro Tries Custom Pre and Freaks Out — rejected
- **Reason:** Overacted generic UGC trope. No missing question or sales logic.
- **Fix direction:** Give the gym bro a specific split, tolerance, and objection.

## 19. Unlock Your Potential With Controlled Chaos — rejected
- **Reason:** Banned corporate filler. No filming concept, no mechanism, no customer profile.
- **Fix direction:** Replace with concrete gym language: late lift, leg day, top set, pump, crash, flavor, caffeine tolerance.

## 20. Doctor Explains Why Your Pre Is Bad — rejected
- **Reason:** Requires authority the founder may not have and risks medical framing.
- **Fix direction:** Use “Founder whiteboard” or “coach-style breakdown” without medical authority claims.

# Approved ideas to film first

## 1. Leg Day Formula Fantasy Draft
- **Concept name:** Leg Day Formula Fantasy Draft
- **Hook:** “First pick in the leg day formula draft is not more caffeine.”
- **Creative mechanism:** Fantasy draft board where the founder drafts formula priorities for a high-volume leg day.
- **Customer profile:** 21-year-old college lifter on push/pull/legs, trains legs after class, wants pump and endurance without feeling cracked out.
- **Missing question:** “What are you training today, and do you need pump/endurance more than raw stim?”
- **Formula priority:** Pump support and endurance support first; moderate energy; flavor that is easy to drink before a brutal session.
- **Full script:**
  - “Controlled Chaos leg day draft. First pick: pump support. Second pick: endurance support, because five sets of squats and walking lunges do not care about your ego. Third pick: moderate energy. Notice caffeine did not go first. If you train legs after class and still need to sleep tonight, the formula should know that. Build your formula before your next leg day.”
- **Shot list:**
  1. Whiteboard titled “Leg Day Draft.”
  2. Founder writes Pick 1: Pump.
  3. Quick squat rack or leg press clip.
  4. Founder writes Pick 2: Endurance.
  5. Product tub beside draft board.
  6. Phone screen showing quiz inputs.
- **Text overlays:** “Leg Day Draft”; “Pick 1: Pump Support”; “Pick 2: Endurance”; “Caffeine is not always pick 1”; “Build your formula.”
- **Caption:** “Drop your split and I’ll draft the formula priorities.”
- **CTA:** “Build your formula.”
- **Props/location:** Gym, whiteboard or paper draft board, marker, product tub, phone quiz screen.
- **Sales logic:** This sells because the viewer sees Controlled Chaos asks what they are training before deciding whether pump, endurance, focus, or stim should lead.
- **Claim-safety notes:** Uses “support” and “priority”; no guaranteed pump, PR, fat loss, medical, or stimulant safety claims.
- **Filming difficulty:** Easy.
- **View potential score:** 9/10
- **Comment/share potential score:** 9/10
- **Sales potential score:** 8/10
- **Ease of filming score:** 9/10
