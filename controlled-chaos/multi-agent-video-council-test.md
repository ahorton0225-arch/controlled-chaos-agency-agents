# Multi-Agent Video Council Test

## Input
- **Goal:** Generate video ideas that can bring views, interactions, and sales for Controlled Chaos.
- **Customer objection:** “AI pre-workout sounds like a gimmick.”
- **Gym culture truth:** “Everyone claims their training is different, but most people still take the same scoop as everyone else.”
- **Founder belief:** “Pre-workout should be built around the session, not just the label.”
- **Product truth:** Controlled Chaos asks about workout split, session type, stim tolerance, pump/focus/endurance goals, flavor, and crash sensitivity before generating the formula.
- **Filming constraints:** Phone, product tub, gym, whiteboard/cards, quiz screen, one person, simple editing.

## Council insight pass
- **Target viewer:** 18–30 serious recreational lifter, college lifter, powerbuilder, bodybuilder, or athlete who thinks supplement ads are fake but still cares about better gym sessions.
- **Emotional trigger:** “I train differently, but my pre-workout treats me like everyone else.”
- **Hidden frustration:** They want personalization but do not want to look like they fell for fake AI hype.
- **Identity/status angle:** Serious lifters make training-specific decisions; NPC lifters buy the loudest label.
- **Belief to challenge:** The strongest-looking tub is not automatically the smartest choice for every session.
- **Comment trigger:** Ask viewers to post their split, training time, caffeine tolerance, or whether a skit “convicted” them.
- **Sales trigger:** Show the quiz asking a question their current pre never asked.

# Round 7: Full scripts for top 3 only

## Top 1: Same Scoop Hypocrisy Mirror
- **Exact hook:** “Everyone says their training is different… then steals the same scoop from their roommate.”
- **Concept:** Founder faces mirror/cards as two versions of the same lifter: “my split is unique” vs “same scoop as everyone else.”
- **Agent criticisms:**
  - TikTok/content: Make the hypocrisy hit in the first second, not after explanation.
  - Customer insight: The tension is status: serious lifters hate looking generic.
  - Paid/conversion: Must show the quiz questions, or it is only a joke.
  - Brand/trust: Keep it sharp, not goofy mirror acting.
  - Compliance: Do not claim the custom formula improves results; say it is built around inputs.
- **Revised final concept:** Use two cards, not a complicated mirror scene: “My training is different” card gets slammed next to “same scoop as roommate” card, then founder writes the missing quiz questions.
- **Why it survived:** It exposes the exact gym-culture contradiction and makes Controlled Chaos the answer without saying “AI pre-workout” as the hook.
- **0–15 second timeline:**
  - 0–1s: Slam card: “MY TRAINING IS DIFFERENT.”
  - 1–3s: Slam second card: “SAME SCOOP AS MY ROOMMATE.”
  - 3–6s: Founder: “That is the contradiction.”
  - 6–10s: Write missing questions: split, session, tolerance, pump/focus/endurance, crash.
  - 10–15s: Show quiz screen and tub: “Controlled Chaos starts here.”
- **Script:** “Everyone says their training is different… then takes the same scoop as their roommate. That is the contradiction. Controlled Chaos starts with the questions the tub never asked: split, session, stim tolerance, pump, focus, endurance, flavor, crash sensitivity. If your training is actually different, build from that.”
- **Shot list:** Card slam; second card slam; founder close-up; whiteboard questions; quiz screen; tub beside cards.
- **Text overlays:** “Training is different?” / “Same scoop?” / “The contradiction.” / “Questions the tub never asked.” / “Build from your inputs.”
- **Caption:** “Be honest: do you train differently but take the same scoop?”
- **CTA:** “Build your formula.”
- **Comment-bait line:** “Drop your split and what scoop your gym all uses.”
- **Claim-safety notes:** Uses “build from inputs,” no outcome guarantee.
- **Filming checklist:** Cards readable; first card slam in first second; quiz screen visible; no generic “personalized” line; tub appears after logic.

## Top 2: Formula Court: Label vs Session
- **Exact hook:** “The label is on trial for ignoring leg day.”
- **Concept:** Product-label claims are prosecuted for never asking the customer’s session type, tolerance, or goal.
- **Agent criticisms:**
  - TikTok/content: The defendant must be visible instantly.
  - Customer insight: Make the viewer feel smart for noticing label hype.
  - Paid/conversion: Verdict must connect to quiz/formula, not just roast labels.
  - Production: One-person courtroom works with cards and shaker gavel.
  - Compliance: Avoid saying other products are unsafe or ineffective.
- **Revised final concept:** The “label” is a paper defendant with vague claims. Evidence cards reveal missing quiz inputs.
- **Why it survived:** It combines conflict, humor, claim restraint, and product clarity.
- **0–15 second timeline:**
  - 0–1s: Shaker gavel slam.
  - 1–3s: “The label is on trial for ignoring leg day.”
  - 3–6s: Evidence card: “Never asked session type.”
  - 6–10s: Evidence cards: “Never asked tolerance/crash/flavor.”
  - 10–15s: Verdict: “Build around the session.”
- **Script:** “Court is in session. The label is charged with ignoring leg day. Evidence: it yelled energy, pump, focus… but never asked session type, stim tolerance, crash sensitivity, or flavor. Controlled Chaos asks those questions before building the formula. Verdict: build around the session, not the label.”
- **Shot list:** Shaker gavel; paper label defendant; evidence cards; quiz screen; verdict card; product tub.
- **Text overlays:** “GYM COURT” / “Charge: ignoring leg day” / “Evidence: missing questions” / “Verdict: build around the session.”
- **Caption:** “What question should every pre-workout ask first?”
- **CTA:** “Take the quiz.”
- **Comment-bait line:** “Is the label guilty?”
- **Claim-safety notes:** Critiques missing context, not competitor safety or efficacy.
- **Filming checklist:** Gavel sound; evidence cards readable; no medical/performance claims; quiz appears by second 12.

## Top 3: AI Gimmick Detector Wand
- **Exact hook:** “This shaker detects fake AI supplement ideas.”
- **Concept:** Founder uses a shaker as a detector that beeps at fake AI hype and stops when real quiz inputs appear.
- **Agent criticisms:**
  - TikTok/content: The prop makes AI skepticism visual.
  - Customer insight: Agree with the skeptic first; do not defend AI hype.
  - Paid/conversion: Must show why AI is useful only after customer inputs.
  - Brand/trust: Keep it deadpan and sharp, not childish.
  - Compliance: Do not imply the detector proves anything scientific.
- **Revised final concept:** The shaker “beeps” at phrases like “AI magic formula,” then goes quiet when the founder writes real inputs.
- **Why it survived:** It attacks the AI gimmick objection directly while making the product mechanism visual.
- **0–15 second timeline:**
  - 0–1s: Shaker points at card: “AI MAGIC PRE.” Beep.
  - 1–3s: Founder: “Yeah, that sounds fake.”
  - 3–6s: Point at “revolutionary formula.” Beep.
  - 6–10s: Write real inputs: split, session, tolerance, goals, flavor, crash.
  - 10–15s: Beep stops; show quiz screen and tub.
- **Script:** “This shaker detects fake AI supplement ideas. ‘AI magic pre?’ Beep. ‘Revolutionary formula?’ Beep. Yeah, that sounds fake. What does not sound fake is asking what you train, when you train, what you tolerate, and what you want prioritized. That is where Controlled Chaos starts.”
- **Shot list:** Shaker detector close-up; fake AI cards; founder reaction; whiteboard inputs; quiz screen; tub.
- **Text overlays:** “FAKE AI DETECTOR” / “AI magic? Beep.” / “Real inputs: split, session, tolerance, goals” / “Build from that.”
- **Caption:** “AI is only useful if it starts with your actual training.”
- **CTA:** “Build your formula.”
- **Comment-bait line:** “What AI supplement phrase sets off the detector?”
- **Claim-safety notes:** Shaker is a joke prop; no scientific implication; use “starts with inputs.”
- **Filming checklist:** Beep captions; fake phrases clearly rejected; real inputs visible; no “AI will make you” claims.
